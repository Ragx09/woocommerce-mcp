# TATVA --- Brand & Product Definition

**Status:** Current working concept for the V1 storefront demo (may change)\
**Brand name:** TATVA\
**Pronunciation:** TUT-vuh\
**Primary tagline:** The Essence of Good Design.\
**Supporting campaign line:** Discover brands with meaning.\
**Brand character:** Premium, considered, contemporary Indian, warm,
trustworthy.

## 1. Brand decision

TATVA is the chosen name for a curated, multi-brand Indian lifestyle
store. It brings distinctive independent brands together in one coherent
shopping experience. Customers shop and check out on TATVA; they are not
redirected to each brand's external website.

The name draws on the Sanskrit-derived word *tattva*, commonly
associated with essence, principle, or fundamental nature. The brand
uses this as inspiration for considered product selection---not as a
claim that every product is traditional, handmade, or sustainable.

### Brand proposition

**A considered collection of distinctive Indian lifestyle brands,
brought together in one place.**

TATVA helps customers discover well-designed, useful products from
independent brands without having to search across many separate
storefronts.

### Tagline hierarchy

-   **Primary brand line:** The Essence of Good Design.
-   **Customer-facing campaign line:** Discover brands with meaning.
-   **Optional short expression:** Thoughtful. Indian. Timeless.

Use the primary brand line consistently in brand-led moments. Use the
campaign line for discovery-focused homepage or marketing copy.
"Thoughtful. Indian. Timeless." is a supporting expression, not a claim
that every item is timeless or made in India; product origin should be
shown accurately.

## 2. Positioning and audience

### Positioning statement

For design-aware shoppers who want useful, distinctive products and want
to discover independent Indian brands, TATVA is a curated lifestyle
destination that makes discovery and purchasing simpler. Unlike an
unfiltered marketplace, TATVA applies a consistent selection standard
and presents products through clear information, brand stories, and a
unified checkout.

### Core audience

-   People furnishing or improving their homes with distinctive everyday
    objects.
-   Shoppers who value design, quality, materials, and thoughtful
    packaging.
-   People interested in discovering independent Indian brands.
-   Gift buyers seeking considered, well-presented products.
-   Customers who appreciate a premium experience but still expect clear
    pricing and practical value.

### Customer needs to solve

1.  **Fragmented discovery:** relevant brands are spread across separate
    websites and social profiles.
2.  **Unclear product context:** materials, makers, care, dimensions,
    and provenance can be hard to compare.
3.  **Inconsistent shopping:** each small brand may have different
    product presentation and checkout flows.
4.  **Sustainability uncertainty:** broad claims are difficult to
    evaluate without specific evidence.

TATVA should address these with curation, useful product facts,
transparent claims, and a consistent buying experience---not with
unsupported promises.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Brand principles

1.  **Curated, not crowded.** Every item should have a clear reason to
    be included.
2.  **Design with purpose.** Prioritise products that are useful,
    well-considered, and distinctive.
3.  **Respect the maker.** Tell the brand or maker story when verified
    information is available.
4.  **Be specific and honest.** State materials, dimensions, origin,
    care, and sustainability attributes only when supported by supplier
    information.
5.  **Premium but approachable.** Refined presentation should not
    obscure prices, shipping details, returns, or product limitations.
6.  **Indian by discovery, not cliché.** Contemporary Indian brands and
    craft traditions can sit together without relying on stereotyped
    motifs.
7.  **One reliable destination.** Customers browse, add to cart, and
    check out on TATVA.

## 4. Voice and copy rules

### Voice

-   Calm, clear, warm, informed, and selective.
-   Describe the product before making an emotional claim.
-   Prefer short sentences and useful details.
-   Sound like a knowledgeable curator, not an aggressive salesperson.

### Prefer

-   "A hand-finished stoneware mug with a comfortable, rounded handle."
-   "Made by \[verified brand\] in \[verified location\]."
-   "See materials, dimensions, and care instructions."
-   "Explore the story behind this collection."

### Avoid

-   "The best product in India."
-   "100% sustainable," "zero impact," or "ethical" unless a specific,
    defensible basis is available.
-   Invented artisan stories, certifications, sourcing claims, or origin
    claims.
-   Excessive luxury clichés such as "royal," "opulent," and
    "exclusive."
-   Fake scarcity, countdowns, or urgency.

## 5. Product strategy

### Launch focus

Start with **Home & Living** as the hero category. It gives the brand a
coherent visual world and supports products with clear functional,
material, and design differences. Add adjacent categories selectively
rather than presenting a huge general store from day one.

### Initial categories

1.  **Home & Living** --- ceramic drinkware, planters, serving pieces,
    textiles, small home accessories.
2.  **Personal Care** --- skincare and haircare essentials with clear
    ingredient and usage information.
3.  **Fashion & Accessories** --- bags, scarves, and everyday
    accessories from selected brands.
4.  **Food & Drink** --- tea, coffee, pantry goods, and giftable
    consumables, subject to applicable food labelling and storage
    requirements.
5.  **Stationery & Everyday Objects** --- notebooks, desk accessories,
    and useful design objects.

For the first demo, give Home & Living the largest share of featured
placements. Other categories can appear in navigation and contain a
smaller, credible selection.

### Selection criteria

Evaluate each prospective product against the following:

-   Design distinctiveness and practical usefulness.
-   Product quality and durability appropriate to its price.
-   Reliable supplier and stock information.
-   Accurate product photography and permission to use it.
-   Clear materials, dimensions, care, and safety information where
    relevant.
-   Verifiable brand background and product origin.
-   Packaging and sustainability details only where evidence is
    available.
-   Reasonable shipping, damage, replacement, and return handling.
-   A price that makes sense for the product and intended audience.

A product should not be labelled "sustainable," "handmade,"
"artisan-made," "organic," or "made in India" solely because it looks
that way. Use only claims the supplier can substantiate.

### Hero product recommendation

**Hand-finished stoneware mug — illustrative price ₹1,299**

Use this as the representative product on the homepage and in the product-detail example because it can demonstrate:

- High-quality product imagery.
- A concise, useful product description.
- Material, capacity, dimensions, and care fields.
- Brand attribution and a brand-story link.
- Stock state, quantity selection, and add-to-cart behaviour.
- Related products from the same category.

Do not invent a capacity, exact dimensions, dishwasher safety, manufacturing location, or maker story. Populate those fields from the actual supplier or label them as demo data.

### Product record fields

Each product should support the following fields where applicable:

- Product name and slug.
- Brand reference.
- Primary category and optional secondary category.
- Price in INR; sale price only if genuinely applicable.
- SKU and stock status/quantity.
- Short description and full description.
- Product images with appropriate usage rights and alt text.
- Materials/ingredients and dimensions/volume/weight as relevant.
- Care or usage instructions.
- Country and place of origin when verified.
- Shipping/return notes where relevant.
- Sustainability or certification attributes only when substantiated.
- Product tags and related products.
- Featured/new-arrival status controlled by the store operator.

## 7. Brand pages and product storytelling

Every brand page should use a consistent information structure:

-   Brand name and concise introduction.
-   Brand story based on verified supplied information.
-   Location/origin only when verified.
-   Product range and featured products.
-   Materials, craft, or production details where documented.
-   Clear link to all products from that brand.

Avoid fabricated founder biographies, workshop imagery, impact numbers,
and claims about local communities. If information is not yet available,
omit the field or use a clearly marked internal demo placeholder that is
not shown as verified public copy.

## 8. Merchandising rules

-   Lead with a small number of strong, relevant products rather than a
    large undifferentiated grid.
-   Keep product titles specific and scannable.
-   Display price clearly in INR.
-   Show brand names consistently on cards and product pages.
-   Make filters useful: category, brand, price range, availability, and
    relevant product attributes.
-   Use "Featured" or "New" only when the store operator has
    deliberately assigned those labels.
-   Keep related-product suggestions relevant to the shopper's current
    category or brand.
-   Do not create fake reviews, ratings, sales counts, stock scarcity,
    or customer testimonials. If review data is not available, omit
    ratings or visibly mark seed content as demo-only in development.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Commercial and operational assumptions

-   TATVA is one customer-facing store with products from multiple
    brands.
-   Customers add items from different brands to one TATVA cart and
    check out through TATVA.
-   The store operator centrally manages product listings, pricing,
    inventory representation, and orders for the demo.
-   Individual brand pages are curated editorial pages, not independent
    seller dashboards.
-   This V1 concept is **not** an open marketplace: there is no seller
    onboarding, seller-owned storefront, seller payout system, or
    multi-vendor commission workflow.
-   Real payment, tax, shipping, return, and inventory integrations must
    be confirmed separately before a production launch.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Success criteria for the brand and catalogue

The TATVA concept is implemented correctly when:

-   The store name is consistently TATVA.
-   The primary brand line is "The Essence of Good Design."
-   The store reads as a curated Indian lifestyle destination rather
    than a generic discount marketplace.
-   Home & Living is the primary launch category.
-   Product and brand information is consistent across listing, detail,
    cart, and brand pages.
-   Prices use INR formatting.
-   Product facts and sustainability claims are either verified or
    explicitly treated as demo data.
-   Customers can shop across multiple brands in one store and one
    checkout.
-   No unverified real-world brand partnership is implied.
-   The V1 storefront remains distinct from the optional V2 multi-store
    provisioning dashboard.
